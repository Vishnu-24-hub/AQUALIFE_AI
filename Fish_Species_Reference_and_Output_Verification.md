### 1. Betta Fish (Siamese Fighting Fish)

![Betta Fish](images/betta_fish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Betta splendens*
* **Also Known As**: Siamese Fighting Fish, Fighter Fish, Plakat, Ikan Laga
* **Native Habitat**: Shallow rice paddies, floodplains, and slow-moving streams of Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam, and Laos

#### Overview
The Betta is one of the most recognizable aquarium fish in the world, prized for its flowing, flamboyant fins and intense coloration. Bettas have adapted to survive in warm, low-oxygen water by developing a **labyrinth organ** that lets them breathe atmospheric air at the water's surface. This adaptation is why bettas are sometimes mistakenly kept in tiny, unfiltered bowls, even though they do much better in properly filtered and heated tanks.

#### Physical Characteristics
Bettas display long, trailing fins in veiltail, halfmoon, crowntail, plakat, and delta-tail forms. Coloration ranges from solid red, blue, and turquoise to multicolor "marble" and "koi" patterns. Males have significantly longer fins and brighter colors than females, who are shorter-finned and duller. Adults typically reach 2.5 to 3 inches in body length, not counting fin length.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 5 Gallons (with a tight-fitting lid, as bettas are excellent jumpers)
* **Water Temperature**: 76°F – 80°F (24°C – 27°C)
* **Water Parameters**: Gently filtered (dislikes strong currents that damage fins). Live, silk, or floating plants replicate their natural habitat and provide surface resting spots.

#### Diet & Feeding
Carnivorous. Requires a protein-rich diet of high-quality betta pellets supplemented with frozen or live bloodworms, brine shrimp, and daphnia. Overfeeding is a common problem leading to bloating and constipation; feed small amounts once or twice daily.

#### Breeding Behavior
The male builds a bubble nest at the water's surface, entices the female into an embrace to release eggs, collects the eggs in his mouth, and places them in the nest. The male guards the nest and fry alone; the female must be removed afterward to prevent aggression.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Male bettas are highly territorial and will fight other male bettas to the death if housed together. They may also attack fish with long flowing fins or fin-nipping species (like tiger barbs). Best kept alone or with calm, fin-safe tank mates such as snails, shrimp (with caution), or small bottom dwellers like corydoras.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Extremely colorful and visually striking, making them a centerpiece fish even in small tanks.
* Can thrive in smaller tank setups (5+ gallons), suitable for limited spaces.
* Hardy and tolerant of a range of water conditions compared to many tropical fish.
* Low bioload compared to larger fish, meaning less waste and easier water maintenance.
* Shows individual personality and can recognize their owner, often greeting them at the glass.
* Relatively inexpensive to purchase and maintain.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Males cannot be housed with other male bettas or fish resembling bettas; aggression can be fatal.
* Long fins are prone to tearing, nipping, and fin rot if water quality is poor.
* Often mistakenly kept in bowls without heaters or filters, leading to poor health and shortened lifespan.
* Picky eaters that can develop constipation and swim bladder issues from overfeeding.
* Sensitive to sudden temperature swings and ammonia spikes despite their reputation for hardiness.
* Short lifespan compared to some other popular aquarium fish (typically 2–4 years).

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Fin Rot**: Bacterial/fungal infection causing fin edges to fray, turn brown or black, and rot away. Treated with water changes, aquarium salt baths, and erythromycin or minocycline.
* **Ich (White Spot Disease)**: Caused by *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*, presents as tiny white salt-like spots covering the body and fins. Treated by raising temperature and copper/malachite green medications.
* **Velvet Disease**: Parasitic infection (*Oodinium*) creating a fine gold/rust dust coating. Treated with anti-parasitic medication, salt baths, and tank blackout.
* **Popeye**: Eye bulges outward abnormally due to bacterial infection or fluid buildup behind the eye. Treated with clean water and antibacterial medication.
* **Dropsy**: Internal bacterial infection causing severe body swelling and pinecone-like raised scales. Treated with isolation, Epsom salt baths, and early antibiotics.
* **Swim Bladder Disorder**: Caused by overfeeding, constipation, or bacterial infection, resulting in abnormal floating/sinking. Resolved by fasting 24–48 hours followed by daphnia.

### 2. Neon Tetra

![Neon Tetra](images/neon_tetra.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Paracheirodon innesi*
* **Also Known As**: Neon Fish, Neon, Blue Neon Tetra
* **Native Habitat**: Clear and blackwater tributaries of the Amazon basin in South America (Peru, Colombia, Brazil)

#### Overview
Neon tetras are small, peaceful schooling fish whose brilliant iridescent blue horizontal stripe combined with a bright red lower body has made them one of the most popular community fish in the aquarium hobby for decades.

#### Physical Characteristics
Neon tetras typically reach 1 to 1.5 inches in length. Their defining feature is a neon-blue glowing stripe running from nose to tail, paired with a vivid red stripe along the lower half of the body from middle to tail. Slender and torpedo-shaped for fast schooling movement.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 10 Gallons (Must be kept in groups of 6 or more)
* **Water Temperature**: 70°F – 81°F (21°C – 27°C)
* **Water Parameters**: pH 6.0 – 7.0 (Soft, slightly acidic water mimicking blackwater origins), subdued lighting, dense planting.

#### Diet & Feeding
Omnivorous. High-quality flake food or micro pellets as a staple, supplemented with small live or frozen foods such as baby brine shrimp, daphnia, and micro worms.

#### Breeding Behavior
Challenging in home aquariums; requires very soft, acidic, dim water. Adults will eat their own eggs if not removed; breeders use bare-bottom tanks with mesh or marbles on the substrate.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Extremely peaceful classic community fish. Excellent with corydoras, small rasboras, dwarf gouramis, and other tetras. Must not be housed with large or aggressive fish (cichlids, oscars) that see them as food.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Peaceful and safe to mix with almost any small community fish.
* Strikingly colorful, especially in larger schools under proper lighting.
* Small size suitable for moderately sized community tanks.
* Inexpensive and widely available.
* Active schooling behavior adds constant movement and visual interest.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Must be kept in groups of 6 or more; small groups lead to stress and pale coloration.
* Sensitive to fluctuating water parameters and new tank syndrome.
* Prone to Neon Tetra Disease (NTD), a fatal microsporidian parasite with no cure.
* Very small size means they can be eaten by larger tank mates.
* Lifespan typically 5–8 years under ideal conditions.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Neon Tetra Disease (NTD)**: Caused by *Pleistophora hyphessobryconis*. Symptoms include loss of iridescent color, restlessness, curved spine, and muscle wasting. Incurable; infected fish must be humanely euthanized to prevent spore transmission.
* **Ich (White Spot Disease)**: Small white grains across body/fins with flashing.
* **Fin and Tail Rot**: Bacterial fin fraying secondary to poor water quality.
* **Columnaris (Cotton Wool Disease)**: Bacterial infection (*Flavobacterium columnare*) causing cottony patches around mouth/fins.
* **Skin and Gill Flukes**: Microscopic flatworms causing rapid breathing and flashing.

### 3. Guppy

![Guppy](images/guppy_fish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Poecilia reticulata*
* **Also Known As**: Millionfish, Rainbow Fish, Mosquito Fish
* **Native Habitat**: Freshwater streams in northeastern South America and the Caribbean

#### Overview
Guppies are livebearers, meaning females give birth to free-swimming fry rather than laying eggs. Selective breeding has produced an enormous range of tail shapes and colors, from simple wild types to elaborate fancy strains.

#### Physical Characteristics
Males are smaller (~1.5 inches) with large, colorful, ornate tails in patterns like snakeskin, cobra, tuxedo, and mosaic. Females are larger (up to 2.5 inches), rounder-bodied, and less colorful with a dark 'gravid spot' near the anal fin when pregnant.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 10 Gallons
* **Water Temperature**: 72°F – 82°F (22°C – 28°C)
* **Water Parameters**: pH 7.0 – 8.0 (Tolerates slightly hard, alkaline water), planted tanks with moderate filtration.

#### Diet & Feeding
Omnivorous. Quality flakes, small pellets, brine shrimp, daphnia, and blanched vegetables (spinach, zucchini).

#### Breeding Behavior
Breeds prolifically and continuously. Females store sperm and produce 20–60 fry every 4 weeks. Breeders use breeding boxes or heavily planted cover to protect fry from adult predation.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Peaceful, active, social. Compatible with mollies, platies, tetras, and corydoras. Avoid fin-nippers (tiger barbs) that target male ornate tails.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Extremely easy to care for; ideal beginner fish.
* Breeds readily, displaying full livebearer life cycle.
* Huge variety of colors and tail types.
* Peaceful community compatibility.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Rapid breeding can quickly overpopulate a tank if sexes are mixed.
* Ornate tails torn easily by fin-nipping tank mates.
* Fancy strains can be genetically delicate due to selective breeding.
* Short lifespan of roughly 2–3 years.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Guppy Disease (Livebearer Disease Complex)**: Shimmying (side-to-side swim), lethargy, clamped fins caused by temperature instability.
* **Ich (White Spot Disease)**, **Fungal Infections (Saprolegnia)**, **Fin Rot**.
* **Camallanus Worms**: Red thread-like parasitic worms protruding from anus; treated with levamisole.
* **Livebearer Fry Disease / Scoliosis**: Curved spine in fry from stressed/malnourished mothers.

### 4. Goldfish

![Goldfish](images/goldfish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Carassius auratus*
* **Also Known As**: Golden Carp, Fancy Goldfish, Comet / Oranda (varieties)
* **Native Habitat**: Domesticated form of Asian wild Prussian carp originating in China

#### Overview
Goldfish are cold-water fish, not tropical, first bred over a thousand years ago. They come in an enormous range of body shapes and colors, from streamlined common/comet goldfish to fancy varieties (oranda, ryukin, black moor).

#### Physical Characteristics
Common/comet goldfish have slim torpedo bodies growing over 12 inches. Fancy varieties have egg-shaped bodies, double tails, and hood growths (*wen*). Colors range from classic orange and red to white, black, calico, and metallic.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 20 Gallons for single fancy goldfish; 30+ Gallons (or pond) for common/comet.
* **Water Temperature**: 65°F – 75°F (18°C – 24°C) (No heater required).
* **Water Parameters**: Requires strong filtration due to heavy waste output.

#### Diet & Feeding
Omnivorous. Goldfish-specific pellets/flakes, blanched peas (aids digestion), leafy greens, bloodworms.

#### Breeding Behavior
Egg-scatterers. Males chase females during breeding season, spawning hundreds to thousands of adhesive eggs onto plants. Parents eat eggs/fry if not separated.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Peaceful cold-water fish. Keep with other goldfish or cool-water species. Do not mix slow fancy goldfish with fast common goldfish or warm tropical fish.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Very hardy and tolerant of cooler temperatures.
* Extremely long-lived (10–15+ years; up to decades).
* Interactive and recognizes feeding routines.
* No heater required.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Requires much larger tanks than beginners expect; small bowls are harmful.
* High waste output requiring heavy filtration and water changes.
* Fancy varieties prone to swim bladder issues due to compressed bodies.
* Incompatible with warm tropical community fish.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Swim Bladder Disease**: Floating upside down or sinking. Managed with fasting, sinking pellets, and peas.
* **Ich**, **Flukes (Gill and Skin Flukes)**, **Dropsy**, **Fin and Tail Rot**.
* **Anchor Worms (*Lernaea*) and Fish Lice (*Argulus*)**: Visible external parasites removed manually or treated with dimilin/potassium permanganate baths.

### 5. Angelfish

![Angelfish](images/angelfish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Pterophyllum scalare*
* **Also Known As**: Freshwater Angel, Scalare, Angel Cichlid
* **Native Habitat**: Slow-moving tributaries of the Amazon River basin in South America

#### Overview
Freshwater angelfish are cichlids with tall, disc-like bodies and long trailing fins. Their cichlid heritage gives them more territorial and predatory instincts than typical community fish.

#### Physical Characteristics
Distinctive triangular flattened body shape with tall dorsal/anal fins tapering to points, plus thin pelvic feelers. Grows up to 6 inches in length and 8 inches in height. Varieties include silver, marble, koi, black, and veil-finned.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 20 Gallons (Taller tanks 18+ inches preferred due to vertical shape).
* **Water Temperature**: 76°F – 84°F (24°C – 29°C).
* **Water Parameters**: Slightly acidic to neutral pH, tall plants (Amazon swords).

#### Diet & Feeding
Omnivorous leaning carnivorous. High-quality cichlid pellets, flakes, bloodworms, brine shrimp, daphnia.

#### Breeding Behavior
Monogamous egg-layers. Lays eggs on flat leaves or slate surfaces; both parents guard the brood cooperatively.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Semi-aggressive, territorial as adults and during spawning. Compatible with larger tetras, corydoras, gouramis. Avoid small fish (neon tetras) that fit in their mouth, and fin-nippers.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Striking centerpiece fish for medium to large tanks.
* Displays complex cichlid pair-bonding and parental care.
* Long lifespan of 10+ years.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Becomes aggressive and territorial with age.
* Eats small community fish and shrimp.
* Requires tall tanks.
* Long fins susceptible to nipping and fin rot.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Hole-in-the-Head Disease (*Hexamita*)**: Lesions on head/lateral line. Treated with metronidazole and clean water.
* **Ich**, **Fin Rot**, **Angelfish Bloat / Internal Parasites**, **Velvet**, **Fungal Infections**.

### 6. Molly Fish

![Molly Fish](images/molly_fish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Poecilia sphenops* / *Poecilia latipinna* (and hybrids)
* **Also Known As**: Molly, Black Molly, Sailfin Molly, Short-finned Molly
* **Native Habitat**: Fresh and brackish waters of Central America, Mexico, and southern United States

#### Overview
Mollies are adaptable livebearing fish capable of thriving in fresh, brackish, and marine-adjacent salinity.

#### Physical Characteristics
Grows to 3–4.5 inches. Varietal forms include solid black, dalmatian (spotted), sailfin (large dorsal fin), and balloon molly. Males possess a modified anal fin (*gonopodium*).

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 10 Gallons (Benefits from aquarium salt additions).
* **Water Temperature**: 72°F – 82°F (22°C – 28°C).
* **Water Parameters**: Slightly alkaline, hard water.

#### Diet & Feeding
Omnivorous grazers. Plant-based food, algae wafers, blanched vegetables, spirulina flakes, supplemented with brine shrimp/bloodworms.

#### Breeding Behavior
Livebearers producing 20–100 fry every 30–45 days. Fry are born swimming.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Peaceful, social with guppies, platies, tetras, corydoras. Maintain higher female-to-male ratio.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Extremely hardy and adaptable to brackish water.
* Grazes algae naturally.
* Available in sailfin, dalmatian, black, and balloon morphs.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Rapid breeding overpopulation.
* Sensitive to sudden salinity/temperature swings.
* Balloon varieties prone to swim bladder issues.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Molly Disease / Shimmies**: Side-to-side swaying swim caused by low temp/salt deficiency.
* **Ich**, **Fin Rot**, **Livebearer Fry Disease**, **Columnaris**, **Constipation / Swim Bladder Issues**.

### 7. Discus

![Discus](images/discus_fish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Symphysodon spp.*
* **Also Known As**: King of the Aquarium, Pompadour Fish, Disc Cichlid
* **Native Habitat**: Slow-moving blackwater tributaries of the Amazon basin

#### Overview
Discus are large, round, disc-shaped cichlids considered advanced-level fish due to their precise water quality and warmth requirements.

#### Physical Characteristics
Circular flattened body reaching 8–10 inches in diameter. Color varieties include turquoise, blue diamond, red melon, pigeon blood, and snakeskin.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 50+ Gallons (Group of 5–6+).
* **Water Temperature**: 82°F – 86°F (28°C – 30°C).
* **Water Parameters**: Soft, slightly acidic, pristine water with frequent changes.

#### Diet & Feeding
Omnivorous with protein preference: high-quality discus pellets, bloodworms, beef-heart frozen mixes.

#### Breeding Behavior
Monogamous pairs. Parents produce a nutrient-rich skin mucus coating that newly hatched fry feed on directly.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Peaceful but shy. Keep in species-only tanks or with calm warm-water species (cardinal tetras, corydoras).

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Visually stunning centerpiece with long lifespan (10–15 years).
* Unique mucus-feeding parental care.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Very demanding water quality; sensitive to stress and temperature drops.
* Expensive equipment and stocking cost.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Discus Plague / Hole-in-the-Head**, **Ich**, **Bloat**, **Discus Slime Disease (*Costia* / *Chilodonella*)**, **Fin Rot**, **Stress-Related Immunosuppression**.

### 8. Corydoras Catfish (Cory Cat)

![Corydoras Catfish](images/corydoras_catfish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Corydoras spp.*
* **Also Known As**: Cory Cat, Armoured Catfish, Cory, Bottom Cleaner
* **Native Habitat**: South American rivers and streams

#### Overview
Small, armored, bottom-dwelling catfish prized for their peaceful nature, entertaining substrate schooling, and scavenging habits.

#### Physical Characteristics
Stocky body covered in bony plates (*scutes*) rather than scales, with sensitive barbels around the mouth. Reaches 1–3 inches. Varieties: bronze, panda, albino, peppered.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 10 Gallons (Group of 5–6+).
* **Water Temperature**: 72°F – 78°F (22°C – 26°C).
* **Water Parameters**: Soft sandy substrate (prevents barbel erosion).

#### Diet & Feeding
Omnivorous scavengers. Sinking pellets, algae wafers, leftover food scraps.

#### Breeding Behavior
Triggered by cooler water changes. Female carries eggs between pelvic fins to attach to glass/plants.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Extremely peaceful bottom dwellers. Ideal for tetras, guppies, bettas, and gouramis.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Helpful substrate scavenger.
* Peaceful and hardy (lifespan 5–10 years).

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Barbels damaged by sharp gravel substrate.
* Sensitive to copper-based medications.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Barbel Erosion/Infection**, **Ich**, **Fin Rot**, **Columnaris**, **Dropsy**, **Medication Sensitivity Reactions**.

### 9. Oscar Fish

![Oscar Fish](images/oscar_fish.jpg)

* **Scientific Name**: *Astronotus ocellatus*
* **Also Known As**: Tiger Oscar, Velvet Cichlid, Marble Cichlid, Water Dog
* **Native Habitat**: Amazon River basin in South America

#### Overview
Large, intelligent cichlids known for recognizing their owner and taking food from hands. Highly predatory and territorial.

#### Physical Characteristics
Robust oval body growing to 12–16 inches. Varieties include tiger oscar (mottled orange and black), red oscar, albino oscar, and lutino oscar.

#### Habitat & Tank Requirements
* **Minimum Tank Size**: 55+ Gallons (75+ Gallons recommended).
* **Water Temperature**: 74°F – 81°F (23°C – 27°C).
* **Water Parameters**: Heavy filtration for waste management; heavy decor anchor.

#### Diet & Feeding
Carnivorous. Cichlid pellets, shrimp, earthworms, meaty foods.

#### Breeding Behavior
Monogamous pairs. Cleans flat rock surfaces to lay 500–1,000+ eggs; fiercely defensive.

#### Temperament & Compatibility
Territorial and aggressive. House only with large South American cichlids/catfish of similar size.

#### Advantages of Keeping This Fish
* Highly intelligent, interactive owner recognition.
* Long lifespan of 10–20 years. Bold personality.

#### Disadvantages / Challenges
* Requires very large tank and heavy filtration.
* Aggressive toward smaller tank mates; uproots decor.

#### Common Diseases & Infections
* **Hole-in-the-Head Disease (*Hexamita*)**, **Ich**, **Fin Rot**, **Dropsy**, **Popeye**, **Ich-Related Stress Immunosuppression**.

## 3. General Fish Disease Reference & Prevention Guide

### Category Summary Table
| Category | Common Examples | Typical Cause | Core Prevention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Parasitic** | Ich, Velvet, Flukes, Camallanus worms | Introduced via new fish/plants, transport stress | Quarantine new arrivals 2–4 weeks; maintain stable water |
| **Bacterial** | Fin rot, Columnaris, Dropsy, Popeye | Poor water quality, physical injury, organic waste | Regular water changes; avoid overstocking & overfeeding |
| **Fungal** | Saprolegnia (cotton growths) | Injury, uneaten decaying organic matter | Remove uneaten food promptly; treat physical wounds early |
| **Environmental / Nutritional** | Swim bladder disorder, Hexamita, deformities | Overfeeding, poor diet, ammonia/nitrite spikes | Balanced species diet; routine water testing & cycling |

### Core Preventive Practices
1. **Quarantine new fish** for 2–4 weeks in a separate tank before introduction.
2. **Test water parameters regularly** (ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH, temperature).
3. **Avoid overstocking and overfeeding** to prevent waste accumulation and immune collapse.
4. **Maintain consistent temperature** with a reliable heater; avoid rapid thermal shocks.
5. **Perform regular partial water changes** (10–25% weekly) to dilute organic waste.
6. **Observe fish daily** for clamped fins, unusual spots, flashing, or labored breathing.
7. **Disinfect nets, decor, and equipment** between tanks to stop cross-contamination.
